Your records already hold the evidence. DVB helps you find it.

DefVetBridge reads a veteran's medical and service records, matches what it finds to the DVA's Statements of Principles, and shows which factors have evidence on file and which are still missing. Then it builds a claim pack with every page cited back to the original document.

Why DVB exists

The evidence exists. It is buried.

A DVA claim succeeds or stalls on evidence: proof that something happened during service and that it is linked to the condition you have now. That proof is usually in your own records, spread across hundreds of pages written decades ago.

Graham Kissell, a former ADF member in Brisbane, hit this wall with his own claim. The evidence was in his files, but finding the right lines by hand meant days of reading. So he built DefVetBridge to do the finding, and to set out the result so an advocate and the DVA can check it.

Built from real use

DVB was built on real records, starting with Graham's own. It is now in use with a second veteran, and their first claim packs are being reviewed line by line.

It is self-funded, with no company or government money behind it.

What it does

Everything between your records and a claim

Read

Reads typed and handwritten records

Scanned medical notes, service records and specialist letters, including handwriting that ordinary text search misses.

Find

Finds conditions, dates and injuries

Pulls out each condition, when it was first recorded, which side of the body, and who treated it.

Group

Groups records into injuries

Fifteen notes about one shoulder become one shoulder injury, so nothing is counted twice or missed.

Match

Matches to the right SOP

Each condition is matched to its Statement of Principles, so the evidence is checked against the factors that actually apply.

Check

Shows evidence and gaps

For every factor: the pages that support it, or a clear note that nothing on file does yet.

Build

Builds the claim pack

Pre-filled DVA forms and the cited pages in one PDF, ready for you and your advocate to check.

How it works

From a box of records to a claim pack

You stay in charge at every step. DVB does the reading and sorting; you and your advocate make the decisions.

  1. Upload your records

    PDFs, scans and photos of your medical and service records, as they are.

  2. DVB reads every page

    Typed and handwritten notes are read and indexed, with conditions, dates, injuries and treating doctors pulled out.

  3. You check the matches

    See each condition's Statement of Principles, which factors have evidence, and which are gaps. Accept or reject what DVB found.

  4. Build and lodge

    Build the claim pack, review it with your advocate, then lodge it with the DVA yourselves.

The work

Inside a claim pack

A claim pack is one PDF that puts the forms and the proof side by side. Every quote in it links to the exact page it came from, so an advocate or a DVA delegate can check it in seconds.

A real pack built for one knee claim ran to 188 pages, with every cited line highlighted and linked back to its source document.

  1. CoverContentsEach claim, what it covers, and how strong its evidence is.
  2. FormsDVA forms, pre-filledYour details, service and injury dates, ready to check and sign.
  3. IndexEvidence indexEach Statement of Principles factor and the quote that supports it.
  4. E1–E12Cited pagesThe original pages, with the relevant lines highlighted.
  5. A1–A9AppendixEach source document in full, so nothing is taken out of context.

Questions

What DVB is, and what it is not

Does DVB lodge my claim for me?

No. DVB builds the claim pack. You, or your advocate, check it and lodge it with the DVA yourselves. Nothing is ever sent to the DVA automatically.

Will it tell me whether my claim will succeed?

No. DVB shows what your records support and where the gaps are. It never gives odds of success, because only the DVA decides a claim.

Is it legal or medical advice?

No. DVB organises evidence. It works alongside your advocate and your doctor, not instead of them. It never fills in the parts of a form that only a doctor can complete.

Who can see my records?

Only you, and an advocate you choose to give access to. Each veteran's records are kept separate at the database level, and nothing is deleted without a record of what was removed.

Can I use DVB now?

Not yet. DVB is working with its first veterans and the first real claim packs are being reviewed. Donations help open it to more veterans and advocates sooner.

Is DefVetBridge part of the DVA or Defence?

No. It is an independent project built by a veteran. It is not part of, or endorsed by, the Department of Veterans' Affairs or the Department of Defence.
