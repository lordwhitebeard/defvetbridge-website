2. What Evocatus is, and is not

2.1 What it is. A tool that stores and reads your records, matches your conditions and service against DVA's Statements of Principles (SOPs), shows which parts your evidence supports and where the gaps are, and builds a claim pack for you to lodge.

2.2 Not government. Evocatus is independent. It is not part of, endorsed by, or connected to the Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA), the Repatriation Medical Authority, the Repatriation Commission, the Military Rehabilitation and Compensation Commission, the Australian Defence Force or the Commonwealth.

2.3 Not advice. Evocatus does not give legal, medical, financial or advocacy advice. It is not a registered advocate, agent or representative. If you want advice, talk to an accredited advocate, an ex-service organisation, a lawyer or your doctor.

2.4 Not a medical device. Evocatus does not diagnose, treat or assess any condition. When it says evidence is strong or weak, it means how well a page matches the words of a DVA rule. It is not a medical opinion.

2.5 No prediction and no guarantee. Evocatus does not predict whether DVA will accept a claim, how long it will take, or what you may be paid. DVA decides every claim.

2.6 You lodge. Evocatus never lodges anything and has no connection to DVA's systems. You are responsible for what you lodge.

2.7 Forms. Evocatus fills in the parts of DVA forms that come from your records. It does not complete the parts only a doctor may complete.

2.8 The rules. SOPs shown in Evocatus are a research aid, current to the date shown. The authoritative text is on the Federal Register of Legislation (www.legislation.gov.au). Which Act applies to you can depend on your service and on the date you lodge.
